About

// the short version

I'm a Computer Engineering student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign who likes working at the boundaries — where a Python script has to convince a switch to route packets, where a neural net has to fit on an edge device bolted to a factory line, where an analog circuit has to sound good through a guitar amp.

My favorite kind of problem is the one nobody has scoped yet. At Lenovo I inherited a half-finished internal tool from an engineer who had left the company, and turned it into a working pipeline that spins up virtual copies of entire AI datacenter networks. At JPC in Taipei, I pitched two AI systems that didn't exist before I proposed them — then built both. Now I'm doing it for myself: Hoop AI, my computer-vision shot tracker, is live for pre-launch reservations.

I care about proof over polish: LLDP tables, converged BGP sessions, TTL-verified pings, oscilloscope traces. If I claim something works, I can show you the evidence. That extends to how I work — I use AI tools aggressively in my workflow, and verify everything they produce, because I've watched them be confidently wrong.
